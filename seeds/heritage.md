# Heritage seed blurbs

Hand-written notes for "The Landlord" demo crawl. Warm and factual — no invention.
These feed `public/data/heritage_cache.json`; keep the two in sync by hand.

## Prospect of Whitby

A survivor from Tudor London, the Prospect of Whitby sits right on Wapping Wall
where the Thames still slaps against the wall below the terrace. It's usually
dated to around 1520 and is often called one of the oldest riverside pubs in the
city. If the crawl wants "old riverside London" in one stop, this is it.
— Source: seed note / Wikipedia

## The Grapes

Down on Narrow Street in Limehouse, The Grapes is a narrow, river-facing pub with
a balcony straight over the water at high tide. A tavern has stood on the site
since 1583, and the Grade II listed building has kept its long, tight riverside
room. It's also one of the pubs Alastair Hilton has photographed for his print
shop, which makes it a natural writer-trail stop.
— Source: seed note / Wikipedia

## The Dove

The Dove sits on Upper Mall in Hammersmith, tucked against the Thames with a tiny
front room that holds a Guinness World Record for the smallest bar in a public
house. Grade II* listed and much loved, it's the west London water-side anchor
for a heritage route.
— Source: seed note / Wikipedia

## The Old Pack Horse

On Chiswick High Road, The Old Pack Horse wears its Edwardian face proudly — the
current building was put up in 1910 for the Isleworth Brewery and is Grade II
listed. It's a good landmark stop when the crawl heads west out of the centre.
— Source: seed note / Wikipedia

## The Lamb

The Lamb on Lamb's Conduit Street is a classic Bloomsbury Victorian, best known
for the etched "snob screens" that still swivel above the bar — a rare survival
of the period's saloon-and-public divide. Grade II listed and quietly proud of
its preserved interior.
— Source: seed note / Wikipedia

## The Sun Tavern

A Bethnal Green pub that turns up in Alastair Hilton's print shop, The Sun Tavern
is kept here as a writer-inspired East End seed for the crawl. Treat the heritage
detail as a soft lead until it's sourced.
— Source: seed note / Alastair Hilton print shop

## The Nellie Dean

The Nellie Dean of Soho, 89 Dean Street, is a Grade II listed pub whose present
building dates from 1900, with a pub on the site since the 18th century. Opening
stop for the Victorian Soho crawl.
— Source: CAMRA — https://camra.org.uk/pubs/nellie-dean-of-soho-london-128680

## The Dog & Duck

The Dog and Duck, 18 Bateman Street, Soho, is a Grade II listed pub of 1897 with
a rich tiled interior; its upstairs dining room is named for George Orwell, who
was a regular.
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_and_Duck,_Soho

## The Coach & Horses

The Coach and Horses, 29 Greek Street, Soho, is a Grade II listed pub famous as
the haunt of Private Eye and of columnist Jeffrey Bernard — the pub behind the
play "Jeffrey Bernard Is Unwell."
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_and_Horses,_Soho

## The Crown & Two Chairmen

At 31-32 Dean Street, Soho, there has been a pub here since 1736; the present
building was rebuilt in 1929. The name is said to recall two sedan-chair men who
drank here while Queen Anne sat for a portrait opposite.
— Source: CAMRA — https://camra.org.uk/pubs/crown-two-chairmen-london-128930

## The Coal Hole

The Coal Hole, 91 Strand, is a Grade II listed pub built in 1903-04 as part of
the Savoy Court development, to the design of architect T. E. Collcutt.
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_Hole,_Strand

## The Lyceum Tavern

The Lyceum Tavern, 354 Strand, takes its name from the neighbouring Lyceum
Theatre; a tavern has stood here since the 1840s and the present Grade II listed
building dates from 1906.
— Source: CAMRA — https://camra.org.uk/pubs/lyceum-tavern-london-128972

## The Seven Stars

The Seven Stars, 53 Carey Street behind the Royal Courts of Justice, is a Grade
II listed pub of 17th-century origin (its frontage bears the date 1602) and one
of the few nearby pubs to have survived the Great Fire of London.
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Stars,_Holborn

## Ye Olde Cock Tavern

Ye Olde Cock Tavern, 22 Fleet Street, is Grade II listed (1977). The tavern was
moved across the street and rebuilt in the 1880s, and is traditionally linked to
Samuel Pepys, Charles Dickens and Alfred Tennyson.
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye_Olde_Cock_Tavern

## The Old Bank of England

The Old Bank of England, 194 Fleet Street, is a Grade II listed pub built in 1886
by Sir Arthur Blomfield; it was the Law Courts branch of the Bank of England from
1888 to 1975 before becoming a pub in 1994.
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_Bank_of_England

## The Tipperary

The Tipperary, 66 Fleet Street, is a Grade II listed pub in a building of about
1667; the wood-panelled bar is widely reputed to be one of London's oldest Irish
pubs.
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tipperary

## Museum Tavern

The Museum Tavern, 49 Great Russell Street, faces the British Museum. The present
Grade II listed building was rebuilt around 1855-64 by William Finch Hill, and
Karl Marx is traditionally said to have drunk here while working in the Reading
Room.
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Tavern

## The Perseverance

The Perseverance, 63 Lamb's Conduit Street, Bloomsbury, is a Grade II listed
building of early-18th-century origin, refronted in the early 19th century. It
traded for years as The Sun.
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Perseverance

## The Rugby Tavern

The Rugby Tavern, on the corner of Great James Street and Rugby Street in
Bloomsbury, is a Grade II listed pub of about 1850, built on land that forms part
of the Rugby School estate.
— Source: WhatPub/CAMRA — https://whatpub.com/pubs/NLD/6307/rugby-tavern-london

## The Dolphin Tavern

The Dolphin Tavern, 44 Red Lion Street, Holborn, was badly damaged by a Zeppelin
raid on 8 September 1915. Its clock, recovered from the rubble, is preserved
stopped at 10.40pm — the moment of the blast.
— Source: CAMRA — https://camra.org.uk/pubs/dolphin-tavern-london-125491

## The Dickens Inn

The Dickens Inn at St Katharine Docks occupies an 18th-century timber-framed
former warehouse; when the docks were redeveloped in the 1970s the structure was
moved bodily to its present site and reopened as a three-storey balconied inn.
— Source: The Dickens Inn — https://www.dickensinn.co.uk/history

## The Captain Kidd

The Captain Kidd, 108 Wapping High Street, is a Grade II listed riverside pub in
a converted former warehouse, named after the pirate William Kidd, hanged at the
nearby Execution Dock in 1701.
— Source: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Kidd,_Wapping
