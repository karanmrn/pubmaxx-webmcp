### 1.3 UK trade mark position

No registered or pending UK trade mark for "pubmaxxing", "pub maxxing", or "pubmax" surfaced in public searching (Firecrawl and web searches returned no register hits; general web search only returned the IPO search tooling itself). This is a **negative-evidence** finding, not a clearance: a definitive check requires running the free live search at the UK Intellectual Property Office, which this memo could not script because the register is a session-based JavaScript search form.

- The authoritative free tool: "Search for a trade mark", https://www.gov.uk/search-for-trademark, which runs against the IPO register at https://trademarks.ipo.gov.uk/ipo-tmtext (accessed 2026-07-21).
- The absence of public hits, plus the fact that "pubmaxxing" is a coined term, points toward the mark being **available to register**. Owner or a solicitor should run the live IPO search across the relevant classes before relying on this.

Registrability, with an honest caveat. "PUBMAXX" / "PUBMAXXING" is an invented, distinctive-looking term, which favours registrability. Two risks temper that:

1. **Descriptiveness / non-distinctiveness in the relevant class.** For pub-finding or night-planning services the element "pub" is descriptive; the coined "-maxxing" suffix is what carries distinctiveness. A well-drafted application (figurative mark or the full coined word, correct Nice classes, likely 9 for the app/software, 42 for SaaS, 43 for venue/hospitality info services, possibly 41 for entertainment/events) mitigates this. This is exactly the kind of drafting a trade mark attorney adds value on.
2. **Genericide risk (cross-reference U2).** "Pubmaxxing" is riding an internet meme. A mark that the public treats as a generic activity is harder to enforce and, if it becomes wholly generic, harder to defend. Filing sooner rather than later, and always using the word as a brand (capitalised, as an adjective before a noun such as "the PUBMAXX app"), reduces this.

Source for UK trade mark basics (distinctiveness, descriptiveness, classes, first-to-file priority): UK IPO "Register a trade mark: Overview", https://www.gov.uk/how-to-register-a-trade-mark (accessed 2026-07-21).

### 1.4 Priority and passing-off exposure, both directions

**Domain priority is a wash.** Both names were registered the same day, so neither party can claim seniority from the domain record. UK unregistered rights (passing off) turn on **goodwill and reputation**, not on who registered a domain first. A registered trade mark, by contrast, gives priority by filing date. That makes **filing a UK trade mark the single strongest move to secure priority**, and it works regardless of who the .co.uk registrant turns out to be.

Source: UK passing-off requires goodwill, misrepresentation, and damage (the classic three-part test); registered marks confer statutory priority from filing. General principle stated in UK IPO guidance, https://www.gov.uk/how-to-register-a-trade-mark (accessed 2026-07-21). The specific application of passing off to these facts should be confirmed with a solicitor.

- **Exposure PUBMAXX faces from the .co.uk (if a third party owns it):** low today. The .co.uk has no visible goodwill (empty placeholder, no trading history, no reputation, registered the same day). A passing-off claim by that party against PUBMAXX would likely fail for want of established goodwill. The real risk is not litigation but **confusion and a race to the register**: if the other party files a UK trade mark first, they gain priority.
- **Exposure PUBMAXX could create toward the .co.uk:** also low while neither has strong goodwill, but PUBMAXX should avoid copying anything distinctive from that site and should build its own documented first-use evidence (dated launch, press, user numbers) to anchor its goodwill.
- If the owner already controls the .co.uk (see 1.2), both directions are moot and the .co.uk simply becomes a defensive asset to point at the product or hold.

## Section 2: Online Safety Act 2023 (U3)

### 2.1 Is PUBMAXX in scope

Yes. PUBMAXX with direct messages, a feed, and user-generated content is a **user-to-user (U2U) service** under Part 3 of the Online Safety Act 2023, because it lets users encounter content generated, uploaded, or shared by other users. Being pre-revenue, a sole operator, and small does **not** remove the service from scope; it changes only what is proportionate.

Source: Ofcom, "Illegal content duties under the Online Safety Act", https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/illegal-content-duties-under-the-online-safety-act (accessed 2026-07-21).

### 2.2 Commencement status as of July 2026

The core duties are already **in force**; PUBMAXX is entering a regime that is live, not pending.

- **Illegal content duties in force from 17 March 2025.** The illegal-content risk assessment was due by 16 March 2025 for services already operating. Sources: gov.uk, "Online Safety Act: explainer", https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/online-safety-act-explainer/online-safety-act-explainer (accessed 2026-07-21); CMS Law-Now, "Illegal content duties are now in force", https://cms-lawnow.com/en/ealerts/2025/03/online-safety-act-illegal-content-duties-are-now-in-force (accessed 2026-07-21).
- **Children's access assessment (CAA) due 16 April 2025** for services operating on that date; services that come into scope later have **3 months from becoming available** to UK users to complete theirs. Source: Ofcom, "Children's access assessment duties under the Online Safety Act", https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/childrens-access-assessment-duties-under-the-online-safety-act (accessed 2026-07-21).
- **Protection of Children Codes and Children's Risk Assessment Guidance published 24 April 2025; children's risk assessment due 24 July 2025; children's safety duties in force from 25 July 2025.** Sources: Mayer Brown, "The Online Safety Act Enters Phase 2", https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/the-online-safety-act-enters-phase-2 (accessed 2026-07-21); gov.uk explainer (as above).
- **2026 enforcement context.** Ofcom is actively enforcing risk-assessment and record-keeping duties and has issued legal notices requiring providers to submit records of their latest risk assessments (a submission deadline of 31 July 2026 has been reported for providers served with such notices). It is not established that a micro service like PUBMAXX would be served such a notice; the underlying duty to hold the records applies regardless. Source: Ofcom, "Helping small services navigate the Online Safety Act", https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/helping-small-services-navigate-the-online-safety-act (accessed 2026-07-21), and Ofcom online safety industry bulletins linked from that hub. The precise scope of the 2026 legal notices could not be verified against a primary Ofcom notice and is flagged as uncertain.

Because PUBMAXX is not yet public with these features, the practical rule is the 3-month-from-availability clock: the assessments should exist **before** DMs and the feed open, not three months after.

### 2.3 What proportionality actually requires for a micro service

Ofcom scales expectations by size and risk. A "large" service is one with more than 7 million monthly active UK users (about 10% of the UK population); everything below that is "smaller", and PUBMAXX is at the micro end. For a small service that has completed a **suitable and sufficient risk assessment and found itself low risk**, Ofcom says only "basic but important measures" are expected:

1. Easy-to-find, understandable **terms and conditions**.
2. A **complaints and reporting tool** that lets users report illegal or harmful material, backed by a **process to deal with those reports**.
3. The ability to **review content and take it down quickly** when there is reason to believe it is illegal.
4. A **named individual responsible for compliance** whom Ofcom can contact.
5. **Record-keeping**: the risk assessments and the measures taken must be documented and reviewed, even for a low-risk service.

Ofcom also states it will "take a reasonable approach to enforcement with smaller services that present low risk", acting only where "proportionate and appropriate", and is "not setting out to penalise small, low risk services trying to comply in good faith".

Source: Ofcom, "Helping small services navigate the Online Safety Act", https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/helping-small-services-navigate-the-online-safety-act (accessed 2026-07-21).

Key nuance: proportionality **discounts the mitigations, not the paperwork**. The illegal-content risk assessment and the children's access assessment are mandatory and must be documented regardless of size. Only the depth of safety measures scales down.

### 2.4 The children's-access trap for an alcohol product

This is the sharpest OSA issue for PUBMAXX and it interacts directly with the 18+ posture.

A "child" means anyone **under 18**. Every U2U service must complete a **children's access assessment**. A provider may only conclude that children **cannot** access the service if it uses **"highly effective age assurance"** (HEAA) plus access controls that keep non-verified users out. Ofcom's examples of HEAA are photo-ID matching, facial age estimation, credit-card checks, open banking, and digital-identity services. If the service cannot claim HEAA, it must assume children **can** access it and then assess the "child user condition" (whether a significant number of children use it, or it is likely to attract them). If the answer is that the service is **likely to be accessed by children**, the provider must complete a **children's risk assessment** and adopt the Protection of Children Codes measures.

Source: Ofcom, "Children's access assessment duties under the Online Safety Act", https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/childrens-access-assessment-duties-under-the-online-safety-act (accessed 2026-07-21).

Application to PUBMAXX. The current 18+ posture is a **self-attestation** (Pal `adultAttestedAt`, a tick-box "I am 18+"). A self-declared age gate is **not** highly effective age assurance under Ofcom's meaning. On current design, PUBMAXX therefore **cannot** conclude that children are unable to access it, which pushes it toward "likely to be accessed by children" and the heavier children's regime. The owner has two honest routes, and should pick one deliberately with a solicitor:

- **Route A (recommended for an alcohol-adjacent product): deploy highly effective age assurance at sign-up** (a third-party age-estimation or ID provider). This lets PUBMAXX legitimately keep under-18s out, conclude children cannot access the service, avoid the children's risk assessment and the Protection of Children Codes, and it aligns with the alcohol-marketing posture in U4 and with the app's 18+ App Store rating.
- **Route B: keep self-attestation and treat the service as likely accessed by children**, which means completing a children's risk assessment and implementing the Protection of Children Codes. This is more work, and it sits awkwardly with an app whose subject matter is alcohol.

Either way, the children's access assessment itself is mandatory and must be documented before the public feed and DMs open.

### 2.5 What must concretely exist before DMs and the feed open to the public

A pre-launch checklist derived from the sources above:

1. A **documented illegal-content risk assessment** covering the priority offence categories (which include child sexual exploitation and abuse, terrorism, harassment and stalking, hate offences, threats and abuse in messages, intimate-image abuse, and fraud). DMs and a feed introduce user-to-user contact, so the assessment must consider grooming, harassment, and illegal-content sharing paths.
2. A **documented children's access assessment**, plus a deliberate decision on age assurance (Route A or Route B in 2.4).
3. **Published terms of service / rules of conduct** that are easy to find and understandable.
4. An **in-service reporting and complaints mechanism** with a defined handling process and record of outcomes.
5. A **swift takedown capability** for content reasonably believed to be illegal.
6. A **named individual responsible for OSA compliance** (for a sole operator, that is the owner; name and contact recorded).
7. **Record-keeping** of the assessments and the measures, with a review cadence (Ofcom recommends at least annual review and always before a significant change, which shipping DMs and a public feed is).
8. **User safety controls**, in particular **blocking** and contact controls, which Ofcom expects as user-empowerment / safety measures on U2U services.

Sources: Ofcom illegal-content duties page, small-services page, and children's-access page (all cited above, accessed 2026-07-21).
