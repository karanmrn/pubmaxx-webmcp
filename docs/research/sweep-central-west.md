### Product implications (what PUBMAXX should surface)

1. **Guinness-quality layer.** Central/West London's single loudest 2026 signal is Guinness: the Devonshire (Soho), Old Brewer's Yard brewery (Covent Garden), Mr Fogg's Guinness Saloon (opens 20 Jul), the Horsemen (City). Ship a "best pour" / "serious Guinness here" tag and a Guinness-trail across these venues.
2. **Live price reality by area.** Bake in the MA Beer Report regional truth — London is the dearest region (Asahi £7.60, Peroni £7.03) — and let users see "expected pint band" per postcode. West-End/City drinkers care about not overpaying; a £6 vs £9 pint flag is high-value.
3. **"Just opened / opening soon" feed for Central+West.** Dated pipeline is concrete: Mr Fogg's Market Tavern (20 Jul), The Shepherd Mayfair (1 Sep), Nakimushi Notting Hill (Sep), The Imperial rooftop Bloomsbury (summer), London Apprentice Isleworth (reopened). This is exactly the "what's new near me" hook.
4. **Closure/at-risk watch.** 10 Greek Street (closes 8 Aug), St Moritz Soho (gone), plus Greene King selling off SW-London pubs — surface "last chance" and "under threat" states so users visit before venues vanish.
5. **Award badges as trust signals.** The George Fitzrovia (Best Pub in London 2026), Punch Bowl Mayfair (Muddy Stilettos Destination Pub), and the 44 London entries on the Telegraph 650 list are ready-made credibility badges to attach to venue cards.
6. **Riverside + outdoor-seating status.** The Chiswick Strand-on-the-Green saga shows outdoor riverside seating is both a top draw and legally precarious. A "riverside beer garden" filter (Bell & Crown, City Barge, Hammersmith Embankment, Isleworth) plus a live "outdoor seating open?" flag would be distinctive for West London.
7. **Match-day / event mode.** World Cup 2026 is dominating Hammersmith/Fulham/Chiswick pub life right now. A "showing the match / fan-zone" filter with kick-off times is timely and repeatable for future events.
8. **Neighbourhood-texture copy that's true.** Soho = nightlife squeeze + Guinness + queer venues (Coven); Mayfair = discreet Grade-II heritage boozers and hotel bars; City = after-work Irish/craft on Leather Lane & Fleet St; Chiswick/Isleworth/Richmond = riverside + refurb heritage. Use these framings, not generic "great pub scene."
9. **Heritage-building angle.** Repeated hook across the region: Grade-II / centuries-old buildings (The George 18th-c, Punch Bowl ~1750, London Apprentice 15th-c, Ye Olde Mitre, Cheshire Cheese). A "historic pub" tag with the building date is genuinely differentiated content.
10. **Popularity/social-proof signal.** The Google-reviews list (Lamb & Flag 5,200+, Mr Fogg's ~4,000, Sherlock Holmes 9,600+) shows review-count is a usable proxy — consider surfacing review volume + rating so users can gauge how touristy vs local a spot is.
